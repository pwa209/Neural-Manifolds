# Results

## Cross-study prediction was comparator-dependent

The sparse stage-N2 portfolio contained 80 participant identifiers, 386 observations and three held-out study groups. The higher-density extension contained 62 identifiers, 380 observations and three groups. Oslo and Turku material appears in both portfolios, while Wisconsin contributes to the sparse core and Rome Sapienza to the extension. The two counts therefore cannot be added, and the comparison is not an isolated manipulation of channel density or report semantics.

The trajectory profile did not show a resolved advantage over conventional multivariate features in the sparse core. Equal-study dynamics-minus-conventional log-loss differences were -0.0311 for the frozen encoder, -0.0456 for sensors and -0.0141 for time-frequency features. Each original conditional hierarchical-bootstrap interval crossed zero (Table 1). The three-study t sensitivity intervals were wider and also crossed zero: -0.2477 to 0.1855, -0.2037 to 0.1124 and -0.2146 to 0.1864, respectively. These sensitivities make the scarcity of independent studies visible; they are not fully refitted population-generalization intervals.

The extension yielded larger relative gains over the conventional comparator: -0.1679, -0.1890 and -0.1914 for encoder, sensor and time-frequency representations. Their original conditional intervals were below zero. The same three models had much smaller differences against the nonlinear Lempel-Ziv comparator (-0.0119, -0.0330 and -0.0354), with every interval crossing zero. Thus the apparent advantage depended on which conventional representation was selected. The study-t sensitivity interval for the extension's sensor-versus-conventional contrast crossed zero, while those for encoder and time-frequency did not; none removes the small-study/shared-training limitation.

Absolute performance tempered the relative result. The training-only constant predictor, whose probability was determined entirely from the other study groups, had equal-study log loss 0.7235 in the sparse core and 0.6770 in the extension. The encoder, sensor and time-frequency trajectory losses were 0.7453, 0.7308 and 0.7623 in the core, and 0.7122, 0.6911 and 0.6887 in the extension. The constant was lower than each trajectory estimate in both portfolios. This comparison is descriptive, not a test of significant inferiority: no new paired interval was calculated for it. Held-out extension AUROCs varied approximately from 0.331 to 0.556 across studies and representations, limiting a claim of useful individual discrimination despite the conventional-model log-loss ranking (Figure 2; `prediction_diagnostics.csv`).

## Final null controls identified a narrow sparse-sensor temporal result

The fixed extension of the exploratory null audit completed 5,000 nested refits for every distribution, yielding 360,000 null statistics for 72 observed tests. All 72 source families, lower-tail counts and Holm values were reconciled against the final report. The count was chosen after the initial 100-refit results were inspected; the larger sample improves Monte Carlo resolution but does not create independent cohorts or make the analysis preregistered.

In the sparse sensor representation, the observed absolute log loss (0.730774) was below all 5,000 temporal-permutation refits; the temporal-null range began at 0.732741. Its one-sided plus-one p was 1/5,001 = 0.00019996 and its full 72-test Holm p was 0.014397. The sensor dynamics-minus-conventional, minus-nonlinear-scalar and minus-spectral comparisons had the same lower-tail and adjusted values. These are four correlated metrics of one representation under one temporal manipulation, not four independent replications. Together they support a limited temporal-specificity result for the sparse sensor pipeline and this sleep-report contrast (Figure 3; `null_tests_72.csv`).

The pattern was not a general positive result across control types. Sparse sensor absolute loss under label permutation had raw p = 0.006599, but Holm p = 0.448710. The corresponding dynamics-minus-conventional label result had raw p = 0.563887, and sensor absolute loss under representation-phase randomization had raw p = 0.071186. Five of 36 sparse-core tests had raw p < 0.05; four, all temporal-sensor tests, survived the full-family adjustment. Absolute prediction and relative improvement answer different questions even within the same feature representation.

For the higher-density conventional comparison, label-null raw p-values for encoder, sensor and time-frequency representations were 0.5345, 0.3539 and 0.3083; temporal-null values were 0.4477, 0.4197 and 0.8842. Across all 36 extension tests, none had raw p < 0.05 (minimum 0.135973), and every Holm p was 1. The extension's conventional-model gains were therefore ordinary relative to the specified matched refit controls. A non-significant control does not prove equivalence to its null distribution. Nor does significance in the sparse portfolio and non-significance in the overlapping extension demonstrate an electrode-density effect or a formal between-portfolio interaction; that contrast was not identified by this design.

## Session-by-context effects did not resolve subjective-scale mapping

The PsiConnect boundary audit retained 36 within-context session changes and 27 context-versus-rest differences in session change. Eleven of these 63 tests survived the participant sign-flip/Holm procedure: nine session changes and two interactions. Corrected session changes included meditation alignment (-0.4552), Lempel-Ziv complexity (0.2180), reachability (-0.4480) and repertoire (0.7031); music alignment (-0.7255), reachability (-0.5961) and repertoire (0.6727); movie Lempel-Ziv (-0.1921); and rest Lempel-Ziv (0.2175). These estimates mix baseline-training-standardized dynamical units with native Lempel-Ziv units and should not be compared as if they shared one measurement scale. Complete estimates, test tails and sample counts are in `context_boundary_tests_63.csv`.

The two corrected interactions were both movie versus rest (54 paired participants). Relative to rest, movie viewing showed a more negative session change in repertoire: -0.8958 baseline-training SD units, with original descriptive participant-bootstrap 95% interval -1.2634 to -0.5049 and Holm p = 0.00855. The corresponding Lempel-Ziv interaction was -0.3861 native units, interval -0.5139 to -0.2445, Holm p = 0.00315. Figure 4 shows all 27 interactions rather than only these result-selected examples. Several other descriptive intervals excluded zero without passing the complete 63-test correction, emphasizing that marginal intervals and familywise-adjusted tests are different summaries. Fixed context order, eyes-open movie versus eyes-closed rest and absence of placebo prevent isolation of a context or drug mechanism.

The subjective analysis retained all 11 original non-imputed ASC11 subscales, nine EEG features and four contexts. All 396 correlations were estimated with 47-53 complete pairs per cell; none survived Holm correction. The smallest asymptotic raw p was 0.000500 for meditation dwell-dispersion change versus anxiety (Spearman rho = -0.4789, n = 49), but its 396-test adjusted p was 0.1981. This minimum is reported as an audit fact, not selected as a new positive endpoint. The ratings summarized the global post-session experience rather than simultaneous experience during each EEG context. The complete matrix appears in `subjective_associations_396.csv`, and Figure 4 displays every scale-feature-context association. This result does not refute published PsiConnect analyses using different representations and subjective constructs.

## Finite observation changed the estimand and interval coverage

Recurrence was sensitive to the segmentation of identical state observations. For the illustrative repeated A-B-A sequence used in Figure 5, the study estimator returned 0.875 when read continuously but 0.500 when divided into three-sample segments. Boundaries removed earlier visits from the set available to the recurrence definition; they did not change any state label. This is an estimator property, not an EEG finding.

The known-model simulation crossed three lengths, two stay probabilities and two missing fractions (12 settings). Across these settings, median coverage of the long-record reference by the original three-observation-block intervals was 0% for recurrence, 5.75% for exit entropy and 44.5% for dwell dispersion. Evaluating the same block procedure against a length-matched finite-window target improved exit-entropy and dwell-dispersion median coverage to 79.5% and 80.75%, but recurrence remained at 0%. Target mismatch therefore explained part, not all, of the short-block failure. Independent calibration under the known finite-window model produced median coverages of 98.25%, 96.75% and 98.5%; setting-specific ranges were 93.5-99.5%, 91.5-100% and 95.5-100%, respectively. Discrete summaries can yield conservative coverage, and both target and long-record reference were themselves Monte Carlo estimates.

## Perturbation and sensory reports constrained generality

Adding spontaneous sensor dynamics to condition and conventional features did not yield a resolved improvement in participant-held-out propofol/TMS response prediction. Incremental squared-error differences were -0.02936 for response differentiation (conditional 95% interval -0.14888 to 0.07557; 14 participants), -0.00221 for recovery (-0.12901 to 0.12787; 13 participants), and 0.0000251 for sensor spread (-0.0000828 to 0.0001219; 14 participants). Every interval included zero. Only two or three participants per outcome provided paired awake-sedation changes, leaving the within-person drug contrast especially imprecise. The outcomes are response proxies rather than direct validation of the trajectory reachability measure. All aggregate error, within-condition calibration and paired-change summaries are exported; individual paired predictions are excluded.

In the tactile dataset, 17 complete recordings remained after exclusion of one checksum-valid but internally incomplete recording. None of 30 main contrasts or 30 pre-response contrasts survived their respective Holm families; all 15 confidence-association intervals included zero. Missing responses were not relabelled as nondetections. In the separate report-task dataset, three of 15 task-relevance contrasts survived its Holm family in 29 participants: prestimulus spatial participation (-1.9201, adjusted p = 0.01499), early normalized RMS (-0.03914, adjusted p = 0.04196), and intermediate spatial participation (-1.6960, adjusted p = 0.04196). Task relevance and report ordering are not interchangeable with the presence of conscious experience. The complete sensory results are in `tactile_contrasts_30.csv`, `tactile_preresponse_30.csv`, `tactile_confidence_15.csv` and `report_task_contrasts_15.csv`.

## Sensitivities and unavailable analyses remain visible

The 186-entry analysis inventory contains 81 estimated cells, 24 analysed cells, 78 unavailable cells and three without summaries; these entries are analyses, not independent cohorts. Of the unavailable cells, 42 required three independent study groups that were not present and 36 required participant transfer across unresolved aliases. The 555 estimated comparison rows are exported alongside the inventory. A missing result was not treated as a negative effect.

Alternative sleep-report sensitivities were mixed. In the sparse N2 experience-without-recall versus no-experience contrast, encoder and time-frequency trajectory-minus-conventional effects were -0.1026 (interval -0.2180 to -0.0013) and -0.1086 (-0.2444 to -0.0102), whereas the sparse sensor interval crossed zero. In the distinct experience-with-recall versus experience-without-recall contrast, the sparse sensor model was worse than the conventional comparator: +0.0400 (0.0019 to 0.0889). These are unadjusted conditional sensitivity intervals for different report semantics, not replacement primary endpoints. Some nominally large high-density sensitivity effects relied on only one evaluated study group and cannot establish study-held-out transfer. Robustness, ablation and signal-level phase-perturbation entries remain in `analysis_inventory_186.csv` and `inventory_comparisons.csv`, including unfavourable directions and unavailable cells.

Across branches, the defensible conclusion is specific: the completed exploratory analysis detected a corrected temporal-control signal for sparse sensor trajectories, but benchmark gains, high-density transfer, global subjective mapping, perturbation prediction and finite-window calibration did not jointly validate a conserved marker of experience. The negative and unavailable results set the scope of the positive finding; they are not evidence that informative neural dynamics are universally absent.
