# Four-figure draft: original results only

These captions accompany the original-results-only figure set. This is a layout draft, not a revised complete manuscript: the subsequently completed LODE and broader DREAM validations are intentionally absent from these figures at the author's request. All numerical panels are reused from the previously reviewed Figures 2–5; no values, tests, or intervals were recalculated for this layout.

## Figure 1 | Neural trajectory framework and cross-study prediction

**a**, Blank slot reserved for an author-supplied BioRender analysis schematic. It should show neural time series → state-space trajectory → trajectory properties, followed by the questions of state sensitivity, transferability and measurement invariance, without drawing the answers. The empirical panels do not depend on this artwork. **b**, Held-out log loss in the sparse core (80 participant identifiers, 386 observations, three held-out study groups) and the overlapping high-density extension (62 identifiers, 380 observations, three groups). Small grey points and lines identify study-level outcomes; large marks are equal-study means, with participants weighted equally within study. The constant predictor uses experience rates from training study groups only. Lower loss is better. **c,d**, Dynamics-minus-comparator log loss against conventional multivariate features and nonlinear Lempel–Ziv (LZ) features, respectively. Negative differences favour the trajectory model. Coloured marks are equal-study estimates with original hierarchical study/participant 95% bootstrap intervals; grey ticks are individual study contrasts. The intervals condition on fitted predictions and do not include model-refitting uncertainty. **e**, AUROC by held-out study and trajectory representation; the dashed line marks 0.5. The two portfolios overlap and differ in cohort and report composition, so their difference is not an isolated test of electrode density. The constant comparison is descriptive, without a newly calculated paired interval.

## Figure 2 | Null controls identify a narrow temporal-order result

**a**, Technical schematic of the two displayed controls: label permutation and segment-local temporal-order shuffling of the measured representation. Each transformed dataset repeats the complete nested fitting and held-out evaluation. **b**, Absolute dynamics log loss; **c**, dynamics-minus-conventional log loss. Rows show label or segment-local temporal permutation for encoder, sensor and time-frequency representations in the sparse core and overlapping high-density extension. Each row summarizes 5,000 complete nested null refits (algorithmic refits, not independent biological samples). Thin coloured lines span the 2.5th–97.5th percentiles, thick lines the interquartile range, short dark ticks the median and black diamonds the observed statistic. Lower values favour dynamics. Row annotations give one-sided plus-one Monte Carlo p-values and Holm-adjusted H values across all 72 exploratory tests; bold teal rows have H<0.05. Four correlated sparse-sensor temporal-control metrics survive correction (raw p=0.00020, H=0.0144); the absolute-loss and conventional-comparator metrics are displayed, while the nonlinear-scalar and spectral comparisons remain in the source tables. No high-density test has raw p<0.05. The refit count was raised from 100 to 5,000 after the initial results were inspected, so this is not preregistered confirmation. Temporal permutation acts on the measured representations, not on the original EEG spectrum. The corrected result does not establish useful absolute prediction, independent high-density replication or a universal consciousness mechanism. Non-rejection is not equivalence.

## Figure 3 | Context sensitivity does not imply experience specificity

**a**, All 27 context-versus-rest differences in session change, defined as the session-02-minus-session-01 change in each context minus the corresponding rest change. Points are participant means; horizontal bars are original descriptive participant-bootstrap 95% intervals, not simultaneous intervals. Parentheses show paired-participant counts. Eight dynamical features use baseline-training-participant standard-deviation units; LZ uses its native units. Dark points identify the two movie-versus-rest interactions surviving participant sign-flip testing (19,999 draws) with Holm adjustment over all 63 boundary tests (36 session changes and 27 interactions). **b**, Individual paired contrasts for those two result-selected interactions (54 participants each); diamonds and bars show the means and descriptive intervals. **c**, All 396 Spearman associations between eleven non-imputed global post-session ASC11 subscales, nine EEG features and four contexts (47–53 complete pairs per association). Colour represents rho on a shared −1 to +1 scale, not significance; none survives the 396-test Holm correction. Global ratings are not simultaneous, context-specific experience measurements. Fixed task order, eyes-open versus eyes-closed conditions and absence of placebo limit causal interpretation of context contrasts. This is an operational reanalysis of an already studied cohort, not an independent cohort replication.

## Figure 4 | Finite observation alters dynamical estimands

**a**, Illustrative categorical sequence, not EEG: the same states are assessed continuously or in three-sample blocks, which reset recurrence history and change the measured value. **b**, Coverage for recurrence, exit entropy and dwell dispersion across twelve known-model simulation settings: lengths 12, 20 and 120; stay probabilities 0.3 and 0.7; missing fractions 0 and 0.2. Each dot is 200 independent test draws in one setting; horizontal bars are descriptive medians across settings and the dashed reference is 95%. “Block/long” compares the original three-observation block procedure with a 10,000-state reference; “block/finite” compares it with the finite-window target; “model/finite” uses independent known-model calibration for the finite-window target. Cell-level exact binomial intervals are in the source tables. **c**, Recorded finite-window target estimates (filled circles) and simulated long-record references (open triangles), at actual lengths 12, 20 and 120. Small horizontal offsets separate settings without changing the labelled lengths. Targets and references are Monte Carlo quantities, not exact population constants. The stationary three-state simulation diagnoses an estimand and coverage problem under finite observation; it does not calibrate biological EEG intervals or all trajectory features.

Abbreviations: Rep, repertoire; Rec, recurrence; Ent, exit entropy; LogD, log dwell; Disp, dwell dispersion; Dir, directionality; Ali, alignment; Reach, reachability; LZ, Lempel–Ziv.
